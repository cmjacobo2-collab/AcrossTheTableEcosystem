HubSpot vs Apollo vs Clay

Finding customers, researching them, and keeping track of them — three different jobs, not three competing tools

The job these three do

Most comparison articles pit these against each other. That is misleading, because they do different jobs.

One finds people who might buy from you. One researches them so your approach is not generic. One keeps track of everyone you are talking to.

A business that needs more customers usually needs the keeping-track part first, and that part is free.

Who each one is

HubSpot — the system of record. Customer records, marketing, sales and service in one platform. Best for Small businesses that want one system of record. Watch out: Costs step up sharply by tier and contact count.

HubSpot is where your customers live — who they are, what they wanted, what you promised. Its basic customer system is free and holds more than most small businesses will ever need.

Apollo — the finder. Contact database plus sequencing and dialing. Best for Outbound teams who need list and outreach in one place. Watch out: Data decays. Verify before you send.

Apollo is a database of business contacts with outreach built in. You search for the kind of company you want, get names, and send to them from the same place.

Clay — the researcher. Enriches and researches prospect lists with AI, from many data sources. Best for Precise, researched outbound instead of blast lists. Watch out: Powerful and genuinely complex.

Clay takes a list of prospects and researches each one so your outreach can reference something real. It is genuinely complex and it is priced by credits. This is not a starting point.

Look at the ease-of-use row. HubSpot is beginner, Apollo intermediate, Clay advanced. That ordering is also the order most businesses should adopt them in.

Where to start: Apollo finds them → Clay enriches them → HubSpot manages them. That’s how multiple applications form a business technology stack.

How a business actually uses each one

A business with leads it keeps losing. HubSpot’s free customer system. Every enquiry gets a record and a next step. No outbound at all — just stopping the leaks in what already arrives.

A business that needs to go and find customers. Apollo builds the list and sends the sequence. Watch the data: contact details decay constantly, so verify before sending or your email reputation suffers.

A team doing serious outbound. Clay researches each company so the first line of the email is about them rather than about you. Real work, real cost, and only worth it once outbound is already producing.

What it costs

HubSpot’s customer system is free. Apollo has a free tier. Clay is paid by credits only.

HubSpot is the one to watch financially: the free system is genuinely free, but the paid tiers step up sharply by feature and contact count. Know what triggers the next tier before you build on it.

Where this sits in your stack

Now let’s build an entire sales technology stack. Your prospecting platform helps identify potential customers. LinkedIn Sales Navigator adds professional prospect intelligence. Calendly removes the back-and-forth of scheduling. Fireflies captures and summarizes the sales conversation. PandaDoc can support the proposal and agreement stage. And automation connects information between the appropriate systems. Now we’re no longer asking which AI can find leads. We’re designing the technology around the entire journey from prospect to customer.

LinkedIn Sales Navigator — professional prospect intelligence
Calendly — schedule meetings
Fireflies — capture sales conversations
PandaDoc — proposals and contracts
Zapier or Make — connect the workflow

The CEO decision

Are you losing leads you already have, or do you not have enough? Fix the first before paying for the second.
Do you have somewhere every customer lives today? If not, start there, free, this week.
If you are going outbound, who verifies the contact data before you send?
Is Clay solving a problem you actually have yet? For most small businesses the honest answer is no.

Apply it to your business

Count the enquiries you received last month and how many you followed up more than once. If there is a gap, your pipeline problem is retention of interest, not generation of it.

Fix that with the free customer system first. Only once nothing is falling through should you spend money finding more.
