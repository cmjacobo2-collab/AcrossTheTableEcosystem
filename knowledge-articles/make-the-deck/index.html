Gamma vs Canva vs Beautiful.ai

The job these three do

You need a deck. A proposal for a customer, a pitch for a bank, a short talk at a networking group. You open PowerPoint, you move a text box three pixels left, and ninety minutes later you have four slides and no content.

The time is not going into the thinking. The thinking was done before you opened the laptop. It is going into making it look acceptable.

All three of these tools attack that gap, and they attack it from different directions.

Who each one is

Gamma — the fast one. Turns a prompt or a doc into a presentation you can keep editing. Best for First drafts of decks when the thinking is done and the slides aren’t. Watch out: House style takes work. Set a template before the team uses it.

You give Gamma a document or a few sentences and it comes back with a whole deck. You then edit it. This is the one to use when the thinking is finished and the slides are not.

Canva — the everything one. Design everything else — decks, social posts, flyers, simple video — with AI help built in. Best for Small teams with no designer who still need consistent-looking work. Watch out: Easy to drift off-brand. Lock a brand kit.

Canva is not really a deck tool. It is a design tool that also does decks, along with social posts, flyers and simple video. If you need one place for everything visual, this is it.

Beautiful.ai — the tidy one. Slides that lay themselves out as you add content. Best for People who want structure enforced for them. Watch out: Less freedom than Canva or PowerPoint.

Beautiful.ai lays slides out for you as you type. You cannot drag things into a mess, because it will not let you. That is either a relief or a constraint depending on who you are.

All three are beginner-level. The difference is not difficulty — it is whether you want speed, breadth, or someone else enforcing the layout.

How a business actually uses each one

A contractor quoting a large job. The quote is written. He pastes it into Gamma and asks for a ten-slide version. Fifteen minutes later he has something he can walk a customer through on a laptop in their kitchen, which the competitor who emailed a PDF does not have.

A salon doing everything itself. Canva covers the price list, the Instagram posts, the window poster and the staff handbook cover. One brand kit, one login, one look across all of it. The deck is almost an afterthought.

A consultant presenting monthly. Beautiful.ai means every monthly review looks identical to the last one. No slide drifts off-style because somebody was in a hurry. Clients notice consistency even when they cannot name it.

What it costs

Gamma and Canva both have free versions worth using. Beautiful.ai is paid only.

Start with the free tier of whichever one matches your job. If you make decks occasionally, you may never need to pay. If you make them weekly, the paid tier costs less than the hours it returns.

Where this sits in your stack

Your presentation AI doesn’t have to work alone. Start with company research and documents stored in Google Drive. Use ChatGPT or Claude to analyze the information and develop the story. Then use Gamma, Canva or Beautiful.ai to build the presentation. Your team can review the work through your collaboration process, and a finished sales presentation can become part of your CRM sales process. Now you’re not just making slides — you’re building a content-to-sales workflow.

ChatGPT or Claude — develop the thinking and content
Google Drive — source documents
Slack — review and collaboration
HubSpot — sales use

The CEO decision

How often do you make decks? Occasionally means free tier. Weekly means pick properly.
Do you need other visual work too — posts, flyers, signage? Then Canva probably wins on breadth alone.
Does anyone else make decks in your business? If yes, consistency matters more than speed, which points at Beautiful.ai or a locked Canva brand kit.
Have you set a template before letting the team loose? Skip this and everything drifts off-brand within a month.

Apply it to your business

Find the last deck you made and look at how long it took. Then take the same content and rebuild it in whichever of these matches your situation. Time it.

If the second version took a quarter as long and looked the same or better, you have your answer. If it took just as long, you were never losing time to slide-making and should stop looking here.
