Zapier vs Make vs n8n

The job these three do

A customer fills in your website form. You get an email. You copy their name into your customer list, put a reminder in your calendar, and send them a welcome message.

Four actions, all of them copying information from one box into another, and every one of them depends on you being at a computer and remembering.

That is what these three do away with. One thing happens, and the next things happen by themselves. These tools do not think — they move work from one place to another without you in the middle.

Who each one is

Zapier — the easy one. Connects thousands of apps and AI models with no code. Best for Your first automations, and the widest app coverage. Watch out: Task-based pricing climbs fast once it works.

Zapier is where nearly everyone starts, and for good reason: it connects to more software than anything else, and setting up a simple chain takes about ten minutes with no code.

Make — the visual one. Visual automation builder with branching, loops and error handling. Best for Workflows too complicated for a straight line. Watch out: The canvas gets messy without naming discipline.

Make shows you your automation as a diagram you can see and rearrange. It handles branching — if this, do that, otherwise do something else — which Zapier does less comfortably.

n8n — the technical one. Automation you can self-host, with source-available workflows. Best for Teams with technical help and data they’d rather keep in house. Watch out: Someone has to own the server.

n8n you can run on your own server, which matters if you have data you would rather not send through somebody else’s system. It needs someone technical. If that is not you and you have nobody, this is not your tool.

Where to start: AI creates. Automation moves the work.

How a business actually uses each one

A home services business. Website enquiry arrives. The customer is added to the customer list, a follow-up task appears with a date, and a text goes out saying thanks and when to expect a call. The owner did nothing and the customer heard back in ninety seconds.

A business with conditions. Orders over a certain value need a manager to approve them; smaller ones go straight through. That is a branch, and branching is where Make earns its keep over Zapier.

A practice holding sensitive records. Client information that should not pass through an outside service. n8n runs on their own server, so the data never leaves. Someone has to look after that server, which is the trade.

What it costs

Zapier and Make both have free tiers. n8n is free if you host it yourself, paid if you want them to host it.

Watch Zapier’s pricing specifically. It charges by how many actions run, and once an automation is working well it runs constantly. Owners are regularly surprised by month three. Check the pricing page against your expected volume before you build anything important on it.

Where this sits in your stack

Here’s where automation becomes really powerful. A lead enters HubSpot. Your automation platform evaluates what happened and triggers the next steps. Gmail can send the appropriate communication. Slack can notify the salesperson. Airtable can update operational information. And when appropriate, financial information can move into the accounting workflow. Zapier, Make or n8n becomes the connector sitting between the applications your company already uses.

HubSpot — CRM
Gmail — communication
QuickBooks — accounting
Slack — internal notifications
Airtable — operational data

The CEO decision

What are you copying between systems by hand right now? Write it down for a week. That list is your automation list.
Do any of those need an "if this, otherwise that" decision? If yes, look at Make before Zapier.
Do you have technical help? If not, n8n is not a real option however appealing self-hosting sounds.
What happens when an automation fails quietly? Every one of these can fail without telling you. Decide how you would notice.

Apply it to your business

For one week, note every time you copy information from one piece of software into another. Name, email, amount, date — all of it.

Pick the one you do most often and automate only that. One working automation teaches you more than a weekend of reading, and it is the one you will actually keep.

Your quick review
