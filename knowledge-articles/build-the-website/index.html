The job these three do

Your website is where people check whether you are real. For a lot of small businesses it is out of date, or it is a social media page, or it does not exist.

Hiring someone costs thousands and leaves you unable to change a phone number without ringing them.

All three of these let you build and maintain a site yourself. They differ in how much freedom you get and how easily you could leave later.

Who each one is

Wix — the flexible one. Website builder with AI site generation. Best for Fast, flexible sites for small businesses. Watch out: Harder to move off later.

Wix does the most and lets you change the most, including generating a starting site from a few questions. The thing to know up front: moving away later is harder than with the others.

Squarespace — the polished one. Template-led website builder with commerce. Best for A polished site you maintain yourself. Watch out: Less layout freedom.

Squarespace is template-led, so it looks good with less effort and less freedom. If your site is mainly about looking credible, that trade is usually worth it.

Framer — the designed one. Design-to-site builder with AI layout help. Best for Fast, modern marketing pages. Watch out: Newer ecosystem, fewer plugins.

Framer gives the most design control and makes the most modern-looking pages. It is also the newest, with fewer add-ons, and it asks more of you.

Ease of use splits clearly: Wix and Squarespace are five stars, Framer is three. If nobody in your business enjoys this kind of work, that row decides it.

How a business actually uses each one

A business needing booking, a shop and pages. Wix, because it does the most out of the box and you will not outgrow it quickly.

A consultant who needs to look credible. Squarespace. Five pages, good typography, nothing to configure. Done in a weekend and still looking right in three years.

A business where design is the differentiator. Framer. Worth it only if you or someone near you enjoys design work — otherwise the control is a burden.

What it costs

All three are monthly subscriptions in a similar range, Framer per site.

The cost that is easy to miss is leaving. Content on these platforms does not move cleanly to another one. Choose expecting to stay for several years, because in practice you will.

The CEO decision

What does your site need to do — look credible, take bookings, or sell?
Who updates it after launch? If the answer is nobody, pick the one that needs the least maintenance.
Do you own your domain name yourself? Make sure of this before building anything.
How hard would it be to leave in three years? Worth asking before you start, not after.

Apply it to your business

Open your current site on your phone and act like a customer. Can you find the phone number in five seconds? Is anything out of date? Would you buy from this business?

Write down every problem you find. If the list is short, you need an afternoon of edits rather than a new site. If it is long, you have a real project and now you know what it has to fix.
