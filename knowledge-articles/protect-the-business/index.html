The job these three do

Every business worries about being hacked, and rightly so. Small businesses get attacked constantly, usually not because anyone targeted them but because automated attacks sweep everybody.

These three are AI security tools. They are also the one card in this series where the right advice for most readers is to close the tab and do something else.

That is not a cop-out. It is the most useful thing this article can tell you, and it comes straight from the assessment itself.

Read this first

Not every AI application is for every small business. Do not buy advanced AI to compensate for missing basic security. All three tools on this card score two stars out of five for small-business fit — the lowest in the entire series. They are built for companies with security teams.

Who each one is

Security Copilot — the Microsoft one.

All three assume something a small business almost never has: people whose job is security, and a platform already in place for the AI to sit on top of.

Charlotte AI — the operations one.

They do not protect you. They help a security team work faster. With no security team, there is nothing for them to speed up.

Purple AI — the investigation one.

If you are a larger business with a security function, these are worth evaluating properly — and that evaluation is a different article from this one.

Where to start: Not every AI application is for every small business. Don’t buy advanced AI to compensate for missing basic security.

How a business actually uses each one

What a small business should do instead. Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere, especially email and banking. Use a password manager. Keep software updated. Back up your data somewhere the attacker cannot reach. Train your staff to spot a fake invoice email. None of that is exciting and all of it does more than any tool on this card.

When these become relevant. When you have someone whose actual job includes security, and a platform generating more alerts than they can work through. That is the problem these solve.

The trap to avoid. Buying advanced security AI while two-factor authentication is still switched off is like fitting an alarm to a house with the door propped open. The attacker does not need to defeat anything clever.

What it costs

All three are enterprise-priced and quoted rather than listed.

The basics that would help you more cost almost nothing. Two-factor authentication is free. A password manager is a few dollars a month. Backups are cheap. Staff training is your time.

Where this sits in your stack

Not every AI application is for every small business. A five-person landscaping company probably doesn’t need an enterprise AI security platform. They do need cybersecurity — but their appropriate stack looks very different: a password manager, multi-factor identity, endpoint protection, cloud backup and monitoring first. AI security comes when complexity justifies it.

Password manager — stop shared and reused passwords
MFA / identity — the single biggest risk reducer
Endpoint protection — the laptops and phones
Cloud backup — tested restores, not just backups
Security monitoring — someone watching

The CEO decision

Is two-factor authentication on for every account that matters? If not, that is this week’s job and nothing on this card is.
Do you have backups you have actually tested restoring from? Untested backups fail exactly when needed.
Does anyone here have security as part of their job? If no, these tools have nothing to assist.
Would your staff spot a convincing fake invoice email? That is how most small businesses actually lose money.

Apply it to your business

Go through your important accounts — email, bank, payroll, website, customer system — and check whether two-factor authentication is switched on for each.

Every one that is not is a bigger risk than anything on this card addresses. Fix those this week. It costs nothing and it is genuinely the highest-value security work available to you.

Your quick review

☐ Is two-factor authentication on for email, banking and payroll?
☐ Do I use a password manager?
☐ When did I last test restoring a backup?
☐ Would my staff recognise a fake invoice email?
☐ Does anyone here have security as part of their job?
☐ Am I considering buying AI to paper over a basic gap?
