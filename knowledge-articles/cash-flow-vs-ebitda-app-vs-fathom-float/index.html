What Cash Flow vs EBITDA does

Two views of your business, side by side.

Cash Flow vs EBITDA puts the two numbers next to each other and explains what each one answers and when. Profitable on paper and short at the bank is a specific, common and fixable situation, and the two figures are how you see it.

It is a financial literacy product first. That framing is the whole of this comparison.

The three it is up against

The three products below are forecasting and reporting tools. They connect to your accounting ledger, pull your real numbers, and project forward. They assume you already know what the numbers mean.

That is the distinction. If you do not yet know why cash and profit disagree, a forecasting tool will show you two confident lines and you will not know which one to act on. If you do know, a teaching tool is not what you need.

What each one is actually good at

Fathom

Fathom connects to your accounting system and turns it into readable reports and KPI tracking — the thing most owners wish their bookkeeper produced.

The CFO Club, updated 8 October 2026, listed Fathom from about $15 a month with a free trial, which makes it inexpensive for what it does.

Crucially it works off your actual ledger. The numbers are yours, not illustrative.

Pick this one if: your books are in order and you want them turned into reports you will actually read.

Float

Float is specifically a cash-flow tool. It pulls your bills and invoices and projects your bank balance forward, and lets you model what happens if a big customer pays late.

The same October 2026 source listed it around $10 per user per month, with a free plan and an extended trial.

If your question is "will I make payroll in three weeks," this is the category of tool that answers it.

Pick this one if: your problem is timing — knowing when cash arrives and leaves — not understanding what the numbers mean.

LivePlan

LivePlan builds a full financial forecast by interviewing you rather than making you build a spreadsheet, then compares your real results against it as you trade.

It is the most useful of the three if you are also producing a plan for a lender, because the output is in the shape lenders expect.

Like the others it assumes a working knowledge of what the statements mean.

Pick this one if: you need a forecast and a business plan together, especially for a bank.

Where Cash Flow vs EBITDA is the wrong choice

If any of these describes you, buy one of the others instead. You will be happier and I would rather tell you now than take your money.

Your books are clean and you want real forecasting off real data. Buy Fathom or Float. This is not that.
You need to know whether cash clears payroll in three weeks. That is Float, and it connects to your ledger to answer it.
A lender has asked for a forecast. LivePlan produces what they are expecting.
You already understand the difference between cash and profit. You are the audience this was not written for.
You need it now. This one is listed as coming soon.

What actually makes Cash Flow vs EBITDA different

It teaches rather than forecasts. Every other product here starts from the assumption that you can read a cash flow statement. This one starts from the assumption that nobody ever explained it, which for a self-taught owner is usually the truth.

The specific thing it is built around — why a profitable business runs out of money — is the single most common and most damaging gap in small business financial understanding. Owners close businesses that were working because they could not see that gap coming.

It is also the thing a buyer or a bank will ask about. They will ask for EBITDA, and knowing why they are asking changes how that conversation goes.

The honest limit

Understanding is not forecasting. Once you grasp the difference, you still need a tool connected to your real numbers to manage it, and at roughly ten to fifteen dollars a month Fathom and Float are inexpensive. For many owners the sensible path is this first, one of those second.

Be clear-eyed about pricing too: this is priced above Fathom and Float, which do more mechanically. What you are paying for is the explanation, and that is only worth it if you need the explanation.

How to decide

Answer these about your own business. The answers point at one of the four.

Can you explain, right now, why a profitable business can run out of cash? If yes, buy a forecasting tool.
Are your books current enough for a tool to read them? If not, fix that first — no tool survives bad data.
Is anyone going to ask you for EBITDA in the next year — a bank, a buyer, an investor?
Is your problem understanding, or timing?
Do you need something working this month?
