Midjourney vs Firefly vs DALL·E

Generating images for your business — and the licensing question most people skip

The job these three do

You need a picture. For a post, a flyer, the top of a web page. Stock photos look like stock photos, and a photographer is a budget you do not have for a social post.

These three generate images from a description you type. The results can be genuinely good.

The question most people skip is whether you are allowed to use what comes out, commercially, in your business. That differs between these three and it matters more than image quality.

Check this before you publish anything

Rights to use generated images commercially vary by tool and by plan, and they change. Firefly is specifically trained for commercial use and is the clearest on licensing. For the others, read the current terms for the plan you are on before an image goes anywhere customers see it.

Who each one is

Midjourney — the beautiful one. High-quality generated imagery with a strong aesthetic. Best for Concepts, moodboards, distinctive marketing visuals. Watch out: Check usage rights before commercial use.

Midjourney produces the most striking images with a recognisable aesthetic. It is also the one where you most need to check what you are permitted to do with the result.

Firefly — the safe one. Adobe’s generative imaging, trained for commercial use. Best for Teams who need clearer licensing and Adobe integration. Watch out: Best value inside Creative Cloud.

Firefly is Adobe’s, trained for commercial use, which makes the licensing position considerably clearer. If your work goes in front of customers regularly, that clarity is worth more than a slightly better picture.

DALL·E — the convenient one. Image generation inside ChatGPT. Best for Quick visuals while you’re already working in ChatGPT. Watch out: Text in images still comes out wrong.

DALL·E sits inside ChatGPT, so if you are already there you can ask for an image mid-conversation. Convenient rather than best. Still cannot reliably put readable text in an image.

How a business actually uses each one

A business posting regularly. Firefly, because dozens of images a month going in front of customers makes licensing clarity the deciding factor, not aesthetics.

A business doing something distinctive. Midjourney for a campaign where the look is the point — a launch, a brochure cover. Check the terms for your plan first.

An owner already using ChatGPT. DALL·E for a quick illustration mid-task. No new subscription, no new tool to learn. Good enough for a blog header, not for a billboard.

What it costs

Midjourney is paid only. Firefly is credit-based and included with Adobe Creative Cloud. DALL·E comes with a paid ChatGPT plan.

If you already pay for ChatGPT or Creative Cloud, you already have image generation. Check what you are paying for before adding a third subscription.

Where this sits in your stack

Generating the picture is only the beginning. Your AI image platform creates the visual concept. Canva can turn that image into an advertisement, flyer or social post. Photoshop can handle more advanced professional editing. The final visual might go to Shopify for an ecommerce campaign, Webflow for the website, or your social-management platform for distribution. The AI image isn’t the campaign. It’s an asset moving through your creative production system.

Canva — final marketing layouts
Photoshop — professional editing
Shopify — ecommerce
Webflow — websites
Buffer / Hootsuite — social distribution

The CEO decision

Where will these images appear? Customer-facing work raises the licensing stakes.
Do you already pay for Adobe or ChatGPT? You may already have this.
Have you read the commercial terms for the plan you are on?
Does the image need readable text in it? All three still struggle, so plan to add text yourself afterwards.

Apply it to your business

Look at the last five images you used in your marketing. Work out where each came from and whether you are certain you were allowed to use it.

If any answer is "I am not sure", that uncertainty is the real problem, and it is a better reason to pick a tool with clear licensing than any difference in image quality.
